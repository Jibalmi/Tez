Where others lead

  • Laya where it was trained: AG News (laya-multilingual 0.948 against 0.880) and XNLI, where laya-multilingual leads nine of ten languages (0.794 macro against 0.699). Laya also edges Emotion (0.550 against 0.540).
  • Laya on single-question latency: 23 to 52 ms per decision against 57 to 250 ms for Tez on the same GPU.
  • laya-typed-decisions, fine-tuned on typed-decisions' own training split: 0.766 against Tez's zero-shot 0.704 (0.725 with four worked examples, 0.793 with a probe).
  • Jev's published figures on Banking77 (0.870 against 0.713 zero-shot; a probe reaches 0.860), AG News (0.910 against 0.880) and typed-decisions (0.727 against 0.704).
  • SemIf's published EXL3 27B scores 0.958 on SemIf's benchmark, above the 12B's 0.943.

Head-to-head on Laya's benchmarks

Laya is the open, encoder-based "System One" that claims to beat Jev: ModernBERT-large or mmBERT-base with a small head that scores one marker per option. Its three checkpoints and Tez answered byte-identical rows on the same GPU, on Laya's datasets and protocol. Laya runs in-process with its shipped temperatures.

MASSIVE follows Laya's protocol: 20 options (the gold intent plus 19 sampled, seed 13) and the first 100 test rows per language. Banking77 has 77 options, more than the 26 letters one readout can show, so Tez runs a chunked tournament: four chunks of 20 plus "none", then the chunk winners meet in a final.

typed-decisions beyond accuracy

Fine-tuned on this benchmark, laya-typed-decisions is much closer to the benchmark's own teacher distribution (Brier 0.061 against 0.355) and to its score levels (MAE 0.242 against 0.482). Tez's soft accuracy is close to Jev's published figure (0.575 against 0.580).

Source: BENCHMARKS.md §1. The best value in each row is in bold.

By workflow and question type

By question type, Tez zero-shot scores 0.81 on yes/no, 0.68 on choice and 0.64 on score questions.

Eleven languages

One frozen decoder against two trained encoders, on MASSIVE intent in eleven languages and six scripts.

The English-only probe holds within 1 to 5 points of the 12B zero-shot on European and CJK languages and falls 13 to 22 points below it on Arabic, Hindi, Thai and Khmer. Calibration, macro over the eleven languages. Tez's default temperature, fitted without MASSIVE, raises its error here from 0.099 at T = 1 to 0.146, still below every Laya checkpoint as shipped; over all 51 languages it lowers MASSIVE's from 0.153 to 0.104 (§5c).

XNLI, where Laya is ahead

Natural language inference is in Laya's training mix, and it shows: laya-multilingual leads in nine of ten languages and ties Tez in Hindi. Tez's zero-shot letters sit between 0.65 and 0.74 in every language; its macro (0.699) is above Laya's English checkpoint (0.609) and below laya-multilingual (0.794).

Calibration, order robustness and latency

On constant-option work with a short variable part, such as the voice loop, the same 12B decides in 30 ms. Option order: when the 20 options of MASSIVE-en are presented in reverse, Tez changes its answer on 0.07 of decisions, Laya's checkpoints on 0.15 to 0.20 (Jev published 0.13).

Source: BENCHMARKS.md §1. The best value in each row is in bold.

Four ways to read a decision

The same 2,000 typed-decisions test decisions, read four ways. Labels buy accuracy and calibration: a logistic probe is fitted with a proper scoring rule, so it comes out calibrated (ECE 0.023 as read), and the gain from labels is largest on ordinal score questions (+11 points over the 12B). How to choose a readout per request is in the docs.

Source: BENCHMARKS.md §4c Calibration and decision types; references from §1.

Few labels

How many labels a probe needs, and which rows to spend them on. "Typical" rows are the members nearest each k-means centre of the unlabelled states, the rule tez suggest uses.

With more labels the probe keeps climbing: 0.760 with 100 rows per question, 0.788 with 200 and 0.793 with all 300 (layer 26, 3 seeds).

Where to read in the network

Every layer of the frozen Qwen3.5-4B carries some of the decision; the question is which one to read. A probe's accuracy climbs to a plateau at layers 20 to 28 and falls at the top. The intrinsic dimension of unlabelled states, measured without a single label, bottoms out on the same layers; the model's own confidence, the obvious alternative, picks layer 21, where its letters score 0.269.

Reading early is also cheaper: keeping 24 of the 32 layers speeds prefill up 1.30 times and keeping 20 speeds it up 1.54 times, with no loss in probe accuracy (eager PyTorch; the ratios are what transfer).

The 4B's own letters change their pick in 51 % of decisions when the options are reversed.

Knowing when not to decide

Conformal selection chooses which decisions to act on so that the error rate among them stays at or below a chosen bound; the rest are escalated. It is what tez fit computes for the gate.

The pseudo-label probe shows why labels matter even when accuracy does not move: trained on the 12B's zero-shot answers, it matches the teacher's accuracy (0.703) but its confidence cannot separate right from wrong, so the gate acts on nothing at a 5 % bound. An earlier analysis with Mondrian conformal sets at 90 % coverage: the 12B letters act on 50 % of decisions at 0.853 accuracy, a probe on 66.6 % at 0.884, laya-typed-decisions on 63 % at 0.876 and Laya's base checkpoint on 5 %.

Cold start

Start from the 12B's zero-shot answers, escalate the decisions below a confidence threshold to people, and train a probe on what they label, over a stream of the 6,000 train decisions. Escalating only the unsure decisions at a threshold of 0.6 automates 95 % of the stream at 0.707; adding a 10 % random audit slice automates 77 % at 0.777.

"Whole stream" counts the human answers as correct; "probe held-out" is the probe trained on the collected labels, scored on the test split. Source: BENCHMARKS.md §4c Cold start.

Latency and depth-pruned models

tez truncate keeps the first N blocks of a GGUF plus its output norm and head, so the served state is the one a mid-depth probe reads. Four cuts of Qwen3.5-4B, served by llama.cpp:

Cut for probes, validate per task for letters. On Laya's public suite the 24-block cut lowers zero-shot letter accuracy by 17.4 points on average (the 29-block cut by 3.7). On typed-decisions the same cut raises it, from 0.483 to 0.580. Source: BENCHMARKS.md §4c, the cut 4B on Laya's public suite.

Voice

220 spoken commands over 16 actions, decided word by word by Gemma 4 12B Q8_0 as the transcript grows.

Source: BENCHMARKS.md §3, experiments/stream_policy.py, experiments/residual_check.py, experiments/asr_bench.py, experiments/voice_demo.py.

Streaming, 198 actionable utterances: a naive commit at p ≥ 0.9 fires none at the first word and is right 17 % of the time. With the rule that a confident none on a partial never commits, 98.5 % of utterances commit at 0.918 accuracy, on average at word 2.5 of 5.4. The class-aware policy (opening an app fires on one partial, media and play need two, typing and closing wait for the end) produces 1 harmful action in 198 and 2 false actions on 22 out-of-scope utterances. A collapsing commit bound, tried as an alternative, was neither earlier nor as safe.

SemIf's benchmark

SemIf's public fixtures: 144 authored three-option decisions in three families and 108 perturbations, scored with SemIf's exact prompt, mean-family balanced accuracy keyed by option id, and 95 % intervals by source-group bootstrap. SemIf's published 0.813 for Qwen3.5-4B reproduces exactly in this harness (0.8132).

Calibration recipes

Gemma 4 12B Q8_0 on the same 144 rows. The best recipe is a six-ordering permutation average plus an out-of-fold temperature.

JevBench public tiers

Not a leaderboard submission. Tez has not been submitted to JevBench and holds no rank. The leaderboard's headline uses a held-out tier and a judge tier; these numbers use only the public tiers, scored with the leaderboard's own formulas, so they are indicative only.

Weighted over the three public tiers with the leaderboard's weights (hard 30 %, easy 14 %, standard 28 %; no judge tier available), the intelligence score is 78.2 through the experiment harness and 79.0 through tez serve, the runtime you install. The hard tier's one collapsed family is temporal_numeric (2 of 15): date and quantity arithmetic that a single forward pass cannot do.

Gemma 4 12B Q8_0; the hard tier has states up to 3.9k tokens and ran with an 8k context. Through tez serve the hard tier ran with an 8k context without --swa-full (the full cache does not fit in 16 GB at 8k), so every prompt was evaluated in full and its times are pessimistic. Source: BENCHMARKS.md §5, experiments/bench_jevbench.py, release/jevbench/run_public.py.

Methodology and audit trail

  • One machine, one engine build, frozen weights. Every run writes a manifest with the data's SHA-256, the model path and the settings, and the harness asserts the model path llama-server reports before each run.
  • Metrics. Accuracy; on SemIf's fixtures, mean-family balanced accuracy keyed by option id with source-group bootstrap intervals; ECE with 15 equal-width bins; paired McNemar tests between models on the same rows; latency as the p50 (and p90) of dedicated runs.
  • Competitors on identical inputs. Laya's checkpoints answered byte-identical rows on the same GPU, and its published numbers reproduce in this harness before any comparison is reported. Jev is never run; its figures are copied from third-party publications and marked.
  • Audit. The first draft keyed balanced accuracy on the gold position instead of the option id, which inflated every headline by 1 to 3 points; every number here is the corrected one. Its "~110 ms floor, no prefix reuse" was the sliding-window cache, fixed with --swa-full. Runs that hit the wrong model are quarantined under results/INVALID_wrongmodel_* and excluded.
